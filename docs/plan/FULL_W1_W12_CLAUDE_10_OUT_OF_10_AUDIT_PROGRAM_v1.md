## 6. 10/10 rubric

Each reviewed world, seam, systemic pattern, and applicable product-journey block is assessed against the following twelve blocks. `Not applicable` requires an explanation; silence is not a pass.

### A. Comprehension

- The learner knows what to do.
- Nothing is asked before it is taught.
- Instructions are actionable.
- Terminology is safe at first contact.

### B. Learning depth

- The concept is explained, not merely named.
- The reason is clear.
- Examples and contrast cases exist.
- Decisions are realistic.

### C. Decision quality

- True decisions exist; the work is not shallow recognition only.
- Realistic suboptimal lines are represented.
- Feedback distinguishes wrong from merely weaker.
- Decision structure matches the claimed skill atom.

### D. Transfer

- Surface details change while the mental model remains usable.
- The learner must generalize rather than memorize.
- Transfer evidence is distinct from exact repetition.
- The transfer demand does not introduce untaught knowledge.

### E. Repair

- The missed clue is identified specifically.
- The repair rep targets the same concept.
- The repair loop closes.
- Emotional tone is safe and non-shaming.

### F. Progression

- Each world has a distinct cognitive job.
- Skill gain is visible.
- Difficulty escalates coherently.
- Completion produces meaningful payoff.

### G. Pacing

- Theory, action, feedback, review, and transfer are balanced.
- There is no dead repetition.
- There is no premature overload.
- Copy density is appropriate.

### H. Seam quality

- The vocabulary bridge is safe.
- Prerequisite knowledge exists.
- The next mental model is prepared.
- The transition is emotionally and cognitively coherent.

### I. Emotional safety and motivation

- Mistakes do not create guilt or shame.
- The learner wants another rep.
- Feedback feels supportive and useful.
- Effort receives honest payoff.

### J. Product/surface coherence

- Title, promise, lesson, task, result, review, and profile proof agree.
- The experience does not feel like disconnected screens.
- Learning and progression surfaces do not visibly contradict one another.
- Locked, available, resumed, and completed states remain honest.

### K. Personalization and repair value

- The next action feels relevant.
- Mistake-family logic is understandable.
- The user can see why a specific rep was selected.
- Value does not depend on fake AI claims.

### L. Commercial 10/10 value

- The learner can explain what improved.
- The session produces visible, claim-safe proof.
- The product gives a credible reason to return.
- The experience exceeds a generic quiz application.
- Premium value is demonstrated before being claimed.

For every rubric block Claude must state the strongest element, weakest element, missing proof, confirmed no-issue areas, and uncertain areas.

## 7. Severity model

Severity describes verified learner/product impact, not implementation size or reviewer enthusiasm.

### P0 - Critical blocker

- Prevents comprehension, completion, or correct learning.
- Teaches a materially wrong model.
- Breaks the active route or a required interaction.

### P1 - Material learning/product gap

- Substantially weakens learning effect, trust, transfer, repair, progression, or premium readiness.
- Has specific active evidence and meaningful impact but is not a P0.

### P2 - Meaningful improvement

- Is not a blocker but materially improves learning, clarity, trust, motivation, or product value.
- May be considered for admission only through the policy in section 9.

### P3 - Efficient refinement

- Is smaller than P2 but has real evidence and EV.
- Is quick, bounded, low-risk, and worth solving now because delay creates likely revisit cost.
- May be considered for admission only through the policy in section 9.

### P4 - Preference/speculation

- Is subjective, weakly evidenced, aesthetic preference, or speculative future value.
- Must remain deferred and must not enter the active backlog automatically.

Claude assigns a proposed severity. Codex assigns the verified severity. Disagreement remains visible in the verification register.

## 8. Freeze/reopen policy

A frozen or previously closed layer may be challenged. It is not reopened by a Claude finding alone.

Frozen means no unsolicited work, no generic polishing, and no repeated audit without new evidence. Frozen does not mean that a verified high-EV P0-P3 issue must remain unfixed. A frozen layer may be reopened for one admitted bounded repair and must be re-frozen immediately after closure.

P0/P1 can reopen a layer only after Codex verifies:

1. exact active source and owner;
2. exact learner impact;
3. current runtime relevance;
4. why existing guards did not catch the issue;
5. the smallest safe repair; and
6. required regression protection.

Verified P0/P1 are normally admitted unless blocked by authority ambiguity, contradictory SSOT, Human-QA-only evidence, or unsafe scope expansion.

P2/P3 can reopen a layer only when every hard gate and scoring threshold in section 9 passes.

Reopening is prohibited when the evidence is dormant, legacy-only, contradicted by current source, dependent on invented runtime behavior, or merely a new preference. A frozen-contract challenge must appear in the final frozen-contract challenge ledger even when rejected.

Prior closure is evidence, not immunity. Prior closure confidence must not cause Claude to skip a rubric block, and Claude criticism must not cause Codex to discard accepted contracts without proof.

Modern Table remains maintenance-only. Mascot work remains deferred. W13+ and dormant AI/persona systems remain closed.

## 9. P2/P3 admission policy

P2/P3 are admitted for high EV, not because they are easy.

### Hard gates

Every P2/P3 candidate must satisfy all of these before scoring:

- specific active-source evidence and stable ID/path;
- meaningful confirmed learner or product EV;
- implementation is quick;
- one coherent owner family and a bounded repair concept;
- no architecture drift;
- no new dependency;
- no broad redesign;
- low regression risk with a focused guard available;
- no product-direction change;
- no need to simulate Human QA to establish the issue; and
- completion now materially reduces likely future rework.

Failure of any hard gate means `deferred` or `rejected`, regardless of score.

### Subordinate admission score

Codex scores each factor from `0` (none) to `3` (high):

- `L`: learner EV;
- `T`: product-trust EV;
- `R`: likelihood and cost of having to revisit later;
- `E`: implementation effort;
- `G`: regression risk;
- `A`: architectural-drift risk; and
- `H`: Human-QA dependency.

Calculate:

`admission_score = (3 * L) + (2 * T) + R - (2 * E) - (2 * G) - (3 * A) - (2 * H)`

Admission thresholds:

- P2: score at least `7`, `L >= 2`, `A = 0`, and every hard gate passes.
- P3: score at least `5`, `E <= 1`, `G <= 1`, `A = 0`, and every hard gate passes.
- Any candidate requiring Human QA to determine whether the issue exists is not implementation-admissible before that evidence.
- At most one coherent P2/P3 repair wave may be proposed at a time.

The verification register must show every factor, the arithmetic, hard-gate result, and admission decision. The weighted score is a subordinate decision aid only. It must not override hard admission criteria, automatically admit a finding, convert preference into evidence, or justify broad polish. Scores rank already-eligible verified candidates; they do not override scope or authority.
